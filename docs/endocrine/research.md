# Endocrine system — how it actually works

Evidence grades: ✓ robust · ~ range · ⚠ contested · ◈ model · ✗ cartoon

This is the physiology the sim is allowed to stand on. Companion to `PLAN-endocrine.md`.

---

## 1. What the system is

The endocrine system is the set of **ductless** tissues that release hormones into extracellular fluid and blood. Hormones are messengers. They travel far. They only act where there is a receptor. Very small amounts can move whole organs. ✓

Cleveland Clinic: more than 50 human hormones; jobs include metabolism, homeostasis (glucose, salt, water, temperature, blood pressure), growth, sex, sleep-wake, mood.

Nervous system vs endocrine (OpenStax 17.1):

| | Endocrine | Nervous |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | Hormone in blood / ECF | Neurotransmitter + electricity |
| Distance | Long or short | Always short (synapse) |
| Speed | Seconds (catecholamines) to days (thyroid, growth) | Milliseconds |

They are wired together. The hypothalamus is the junction. Fight-or-flight is neural first, then the adrenal medulla dumps epinephrine in seconds. ✗ “hormones are always slow”

Exocrine is a different door: sweat, saliva, pancreatic juice down a **duct**. Lymph nodes are not glands. Merck calls out the “swollen glands” mix-up. ✓

## 2. Hormone chemistry (why the pictures change)

OpenStax 17.2 classes:

| Class | Made from | Blood | Receptor | Speed / half-life examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amine | Tryptophan (melatonin) or tyrosine (T3/T4, catecholamines, dopamine) | Catecholamines water-soluble; thyroid behaves more like a steroid in transport | Membrane (catecholamines) vs intracellular (thyroid) | Epinephrine ~1 min; thyroid long because 99% is protein-bound |
| Peptide / protein | Amino acid chains (insulin, GH, ADH, TSH…) | Water-soluble, often free | Membrane → second messenger (often cAMP, sometimes Ca²⁺/IP3) | Minutes |
| Steroid | Cholesterol (cortisol, aldosterone, estradiol, testosterone) | Need binding proteins | Intracellular → DNA → protein synthesis | Cortisol ~60–90 min |

Rules the zoom must keep:

- Water-soluble hormones do **not** cross the lipid bilayer. They knock on the door. Cascade inside. ✓
- Steroids and thyroid **do** reach DNA. Slower plot, longer echo. ✓
- Bound vs free: less than 1% of circulating T3/T4 is free; the free fraction is the active one. ✓
- Downregulation / upregulation of receptors when a hormone is chronically high or low. ✓
- Hormone–hormone math: permissive (thyroid helps sex steroids work), synergistic (FSH + estrogen on the follicle), antagonistic (insulin vs glucagon). ✓

Stimuli (OpenStax):

- **Humoral** — glucose → insulin; osmolarity → ADH; Ca²⁺ → PTH
- **Hormonal** — TRH → TSH → T3/T4
- **Neural** — sympathetics → adrenal medulla

Feedback: almost everything is **negative**. Positive is the exception (oxytocin in labor). ✗ a fountain with no lid

## 3. Glands, living placement

Sizes are adult typical. ~ Range. Use living anatomy, not a classroom poster that inflates the pituitary.

### Hypothalamus

Diencephalon, below the thalamus. Neural + endocrine. Makes releasing/inhibiting hormones for the anterior pituitary, and makes ADH and oxytocin that are **stored** in the posterior pituitary. Also dopamine (PIH) and somatostatin. Cleveland Clinic / OpenStax. ✓

Releasing set (Merck / OpenStax): TRH, CRH, GnRH, GHRH; inhibitors: somatostatin, dopamine.

### Pituitary (hypophysis)

Pea / bean in the **sella turcica**, on the infundibulum. Two origins:

- **Posterior (neurohypophysis)** — nerve endings. Does not synthesize. Releases ADH (vasopressin) and oxytocin.
- **Anterior (adenohypophysis)** — glandular. Reached by the **hypophyseal portal system** so hypothalamic peptides never have to wander the whole body first. ✓

Anterior hormones: GH, PRL, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH (plus POMC fragments / MSH in the intermediate zone). TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH are tropic — they turn other glands. ✓

MRI, not x-ray, is how you look at this gland. Posterior lobe can be T1-bright from vasopressin stores. ✓ imaging

### Pineal

Tiny, midline, melatonin. Darkness + SCN clock. Night light suppresses. Calcification can show on a skull film — rare endocrine cameo on plain x-ray. ✓ / ~

### Thyroid

Butterfly, **anterior trachea**, inferior to larynx. Isthmus + lobes. Follicles filled with **colloid**. Iodine is bolted onto thyroglobulin → T4 (more of it) and T3 (more potent). Many tissues deiodinate T4 → T3. Calcitonin from C cells; adult calcium control is mostly PTH, not calcitonin. OpenStax 17.4. ✓

Needs iodine. US iodized salt (1924 onward) made deficiency rare in the US; it is still the big preventable cause of goiter and of intellectual disability worldwide. NIDDK: Hashimoto is the usual US hypothyroid cause, not a sushi deficit. ✓

On imaging: ultrasound first. Non-contrast CT: mildly hyperdense (iodine), ~80–100 HU. Nuclear uptake shows **function**. Plain film: usually nothing. ✓

### Parathyroids

Four peas posterior to thyroid; ectopic possible. PTH raises blood Ca²⁺ (bone, kidney, vitamin D activation). ✓

### Adrenals

Triangles on the kidneys. Highest-range blood flow. Cortex then medulla, blood-wise.

| Zone | Hormone | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Zona glomerulosa | Aldosterone | Na⁺ keep, K⁺ dump, volume / BP |
| Zona fasciculata | Cortisol (glucocorticoids) | Glucose up, protein/fat catabolism, immune down |
| Zona reticularis | Androgens (DHEA etc.) | Weak sex-steroid supply |
| Medulla | Epi > NE (~4:1) | Fight-or-flight |

Cortex = HPA (CRH → ACTH → cortisol). Medulla = SAM (nerves). **Two clocks.** ✗ one “stress juice”

NIDDK: cortisol is essential for life. Addison / adrenal insufficiency is missing that, not a wellness slogan.

### Pancreas (endocrine slice)

Long, retroperitoneal, under the stomach. Mostly **exocrine**. Islets:

| Cell | ~ share | Hormone |
|---|---|---|
| Beta | ~75% | Insulin — glucose into muscle/fat; glycogen; stop gluconeogenesis |
| Alpha | ~20% | Glucagon — glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis, lipolysis |
| Delta | ~4% | Somatostatin — brakes on insulin and glucagon |
| PP | ~1% | Pancreatic polypeptide |

OpenStax fasting band: about 70–100 mg/dL. ~ textbook range; clinics use their own cutoffs.

Insulin is not required for glucose uptake in brain, RBCs, liver, kidney, gut lining. Muscle and adipose are the main insulin-needers. ✓

Incretins (GIP, GLP-1) from gut warn beta cells **during** the meal, not after the glucose peak. Endocrine Society. ✓

NIDDK: diabetes = glucose too high because insulin is missing (type 1, autoimmune beta death) or not working well enough (type 2, resistance ± tired beta cells). Type 2 is ~90–95% of cases. This app does not diagnose it.

### Gonads

Ovaries: estradiol, progesterone, some androgens; eggs. Testes: testosterone; sperm. Driven by GnRH pulses → LH/FSH. ✓

### Adipose and the rest

Fat is an endocrine organ: leptin (long-term energy, satiety to hypothalamus), adiponectin (insulin sensitivity), aromatase, angiotensin. Endocrine Society. Too much fat → leptin high but **resistance** is the usual obesity story, not “no leptin.” ⚠ popular “leptin diet” claims

Gut is the largest endocrine-related organ by some counts: gastrin, ghrelin, CCK, GLP-1, PYY. Heart: ANP/BNP. Kidney: renin, EPO. Liver: IGF-1, angiotensinogen. Skin–liver–kidney: vitamin D → calcitriol. Placenta: pregnancy only. ✓

Thymus: childhood immune school, involutes. Do not default a huge thymus on an adult. ✓

## 4. Axes that must close

### HPT

TRH → TSH → T3/T4 → cools TRH/TSH. Iodine in, colloid store out. Days, not minutes. ✓

### HPA

CRH → ACTH → cortisol → cools CRH/ACTH. Clock + stress. Dawn high, midnight low. ✓ / ~ exact clock time varies

### HPG

GnRH **pulses** → LH/FSH → sex steroids / gametes → cool GnRH. A flat GnRH drip would desensitize. ✗ steady drip

### Glucose

Meal / incretins / glucose → insulin. Fast / exercise / falling glucose → glucagon. Antagonistic. ✓

### Calcium

PTH vs (weak) calcitonin; vitamin D. ✓

### Water / salt

Osmolarity → ADH. Volume / Na⁺ / K⁺ / angiotensin II → aldosterone. Alcohol **inhibits ADH** → extra urine. OpenStax. ✓

### Pineal / SCN

Light via the eyes → SCN → pineal. Melatonin is a darkness signal, not a sleeping pill molecule you can see. ✓

## 5. Time — what “when” means

Cartoons use one whoosh. Real endocrine uses several clocks.

**Seconds:** SAM epinephrine / norepinephrine. Neural. Half-life ~1 min. Pupils, heart, bronchi, glucose dump, gut quiet. ✓

**Minutes:** insulin first phase; incretins; ADH; oxytocin let-down. Glucose after a carb meal often peaks ~30–60 min. ~ 

**Tens of minutes:** cortisol after a stressor; cortisol awakening response ~30–45 min after waking. ~

**Hours:** mixed-meal insulin returning toward baseline; melatonin evening rise. ~

**Sleep-tied:** GH largest pulse near sleep onset. Miss the night, miss a lot of that pulse. ✓ / ~

**Days:** thyroid pool. You cannot “boost metabolism” with one iodine snack. ✓

**Weeks:** HPG / menstrual plot.

Dawn phenomenon (research literature): glucose and insulin can tick up before waking even without breakfast. Do not treat every 07:00 glucose bump as “they snacked.” ⚠ teach as a named thing, don’t overfit

Night vs morning carbs: insulin sensitivity is generally better in the biological morning than at night. Same soda, different curve. ⚠ / ◈ for the avatar

## 6. Food, drink, light, sleep, stress — what actually couples in

This is the digestive brief’s “what happens when you put things in your mouth,” plus the inputs endocrine cannot ignore.

| Input | Short coupling | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose / starch / sugar | Insulin up, glucagon down | ✓ |
| Protein | Insulin **and** glucagon; amino acids | ✓ |
| Fat | Slower emptying, CCK; smaller glucose spike when mixed | ✓ |
| Fiber | Blunts glucose | ✓ |
| Coffee / caffeine | Catecholamine / cortisol nudge | ~ / ⚠ dose |
| Alcohol | ADH down; sleep architecture; next-day metabolic noise | ✓ ADH; ⚠ “hormone detox” |
| Iodine (salt, seafood, seaweed) | Thyroid substrate. Excess seaweed can worsen some autoimmune thyroid disease (NIDDK) | ✓ |
| Soy / phytoestrogens | Weak estrogen-like plant molecules | ⚠ as a health claim, ✓ as a chemical class (NIEHS) |
| Bright light at night | Melatonin suppression | ✓ |
| Morning daylight | Clock alignment, melatonin off | ✓ |
| Sleep loss | Worse next-day insulin handling; ghrelin/leptin noise | ✓ direction in studies, ~ size |
| Acute scare / sprint | SAM now, HPA after | ✓ |
| Chronic stress | HPA, sleep, eating — messy | ⚠ |
| Exercise training pattern | Catecholamines acute; insulin sensitivity over days | ✓ |
| BPA, phthalates, PFAS, etc. | May mimic / block / interfere (NIEHS, Endocrine Society) | ✓ class, ⚠ one-product scare |

Ghrelin rises before meals and in calorie cuts; leptin tracks fat mass over weeks, not the last bite. Endocrine Society. ✓ mechanism, ◈ personal hunger

GLP-1: measurable within ~10 minutes of eating, lasts hours; drugs copy this. Endocrine Society. ✓

## 7. What imaging actually shows

| Modality | What you see | What you do not |
|---|---|---|
| Plain x-ray | Bone, gas, maybe calcified pineal | Hormones, islets, pituitary, usually thyroid |
| Thyroid US | Size, texture, nodules | Hormone level |
| Neck CT/MRI | Anatomy, goiter into chest | Function (and iodinated CT contrast can block radioiodine for weeks) |
| Thyroid uptake/scan | Who is trapping iodine / tracer; hot vs cold | Histology |
| Pituitary MRI | Sella, stalk, adenoma, bright spot | Blood TSH |
| Adrenal CT/MRI | Shape, incidental mass | Whether the mass is *doing* anything — that is labs |

The clinical “picture” of hormones is the **timed blood test**. TSH without free T4 is half a sentence. Cortisol without clock time is a lie. ✓

|---|---|
| Glands the size of fruits | Pituitary is a pea. Thyroid is a thin butterfly. Islets are a percent of the pancreas. |
| Neon arrows of hormone | Dilute molecules; receptors are the aim. |
